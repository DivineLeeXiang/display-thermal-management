Passive Cooling: Phonon Physics & The ZA Flexural Mode
Microscopic Mechanism

The Heat Carrier: Phonons, Not Electrons. In dielectric 2D materials (graphene and h-BN), heat is transported almost exclusively by lattice vibrational wavepackets (phonons), not by free carriers. Thermal conductivity is dictated entirely by phonon mean free path before scattering events.

Phonon Dispersion & Mean Free Path Schematic:
Suspended Graphene (Ballistic)
• Quadratic dispersion (ω ∝ q²) in flexural ZA mode.
• Strict mirror symmetry suppresses scattering.
• Mean free path: Micron-scale (>1000 nm).
• κ = 2000–5000 W/m·K.
Supported Graphene on SiO₂ (Diffusive)
• Substrate roughness breaks mirror symmetry.
• Flexural phonon leakage directly into SiO₂.
• Mean free path collapses: ~90 nm.
• κ drops to: ~600 W/m·K (Seol et al., Science 2010).

Why h-BN is the chosen interposer: Although h-BN has lower in-plane conductivity (~300 W/m·K), it is a wide-bandgap insulator with a high dielectric breakdown field (>0.7 V/nm). Graphene cannot touch subpixel driving electrodes without catastrophic shorting; h-BN touches electronics safely while maintaining planar heat spreading.

Why Stack Them: Two Independent Loss Mechanisms

• Passive spreading loses performance across the 2D/substrate boundary via distributed flexural phonon leakage (Seol et al., 2010).
• Active cooling loses performance at the metal/TE element interface via localized parasitic Joule heating (I²r_c) right at the pumping site.

Because these are physically distinct interfaces with distinct loss mechanisms, fixing one does not fix the other, and stacking them does not make either loss worse. They act on the thermal problem sequentially, not competitively.

The Honest Quantitative Core & Concrete Future Work
Rigorous Scope
What is Fully Derived & Defensible:
• The spreading-resistance model (R_spread = 1/(4·k·a)) is analytically exact and anchored to real Gonçalves leg geometry and Kalantari & Zhang 2D conductivity data.
• The combined couple figure of merit (Z_couple = 2.18×10⁻³ K⁻¹, ZT = 0.65) is computed directly from experimental material constants.
What Requires Experimental Superposition Validation:
• A multi-layer FEM simulation resolving both interfaces simultaneously (extending Gonçalves' single-layer method to include the h-BN dielectric boundary).
• Direct empirical sourcing of absolute micro-emitter heat flux in commercial sub-10 μm pixels.
• Test structure fabrication: Sub-50 µm thermoelectric junctions deposited on CVD-grown h-BN to confirm thermal boundary coupling.
